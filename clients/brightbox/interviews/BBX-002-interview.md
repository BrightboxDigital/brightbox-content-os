# BBX-002 Interview

**Article:** Local Services Ads Are Moving Into Google Ads. Here's What Changes for Contractors.
**Date opened:** 2026-08-01

## Questions asked, 2026-08-01

**1.** Do you currently manage Local Services Ads for any client, and if so, what type of business?
If yes, has Google given them a migration date or any early signal about this change yet?

**2.** When a client's ad account structure changes like this, what's the first thing you personally
check or export, before anything migrates — your own process, not Google's official checklist?

**3.** A question keeps coming up in the real discussion threads found in research: if a business
already runs a standard Performance Max campaign, does this new LSA-flavored Performance Max
campaign compete with it for budget, or run entirely separately? Do you have a read on this, or
should the article flag it as a genuinely open question?

**4.** Is there a real (anonymized) client example, or a realistic scenario based on your
account-management experience, that shows what it could actually cost someone if they ignore the
"export your historical data" warning?

**5.** How common are Local Services Ads among your Fort Wayne client base right now — is this
urgent for people you're actively managing, or more of a "get ahead of it" piece for people who
might use LSAs down the line?

## Answers, given 2026-08-01

**1. Current LSA clients.** Runs Local Services Ads for two clients currently. No migration date or
early signal from Google yet for either. Business type not specified — not to be invented.

**2. His process.** Checks Google's own documentation first to see exactly what is happening, then
reviews his own account setup and process to see what needs to change or improve.

**3. Performance Max interaction.** Genuinely uncertain, and said so directly. His professional
guess: since the new campaign type is still pay-per-lead at its core, he doesn't expect it to
compete against a separate standard Performance Max campaign for budget. **This is his opinion, not
a confirmed fact** — Google's own documentation does not explicitly settle this. Must be labeled
clearly as his read, with the uncertainty stated plainly rather than smoothed over.

**4. Cost of ignoring the data-export warning.** No real client example of this happening. His
reasoning: the real cost is that you lose the ability to reference historical account data if you
ever need it later — for example if a question comes up about past performance. **No case study
exists and none should be invented.** This stays as his stated professional reasoning about the
risk, not a story.

**5. How common LSAs are for his clients.** Not very common among his current client base. He
believes, as his own view, that more local service businesses should be using them.

## What this means for the draft

- Real, usable, first-person material: Archie currently manages LSA for two clients (unspecified
  type), with no migration notice yet — a genuine, verifiable current-state detail, not a case
  study.
- His process for handling an account-structure change is real but simple (check Google's guidance,
  then audit his own setup) — the checklist section should present this honestly rather than padding
  it into a longer list he didn't actually give.
- The Performance-Max-interaction question gets answered as **his professional opinion, explicitly
  labeled as unconfirmed**, which is itself an honest and useful original-value contribution since
  no competitor resolves this either.
- No invented data-loss example. The article states the risk conceptually (losing the ability to
  reference historical performance later) as Archie's stated reasoning, not as something that
  happened to a real client.
- Tone: since LSAs are not common among his current clients, this should lean more "here's what's
  coming and what to watch for" than "urgent, act now for your active campaigns" — with his honest
  aside that he thinks more local service businesses should be using LSAs, which ties naturally into
  the closing CTA.
