# BBX-004 Interview — What Actually Affects Google Business Profile Visibility

Answered by Archie Brady, 2026-09-15.

## Q1. When you're auditing a client's GBP, what's the one thing you check first that most owners get wrong?

Primary category. It needs to be their main/highest-value service, with secondary categories for
supporting services. Real example: a remodeling client doing kitchens, bathrooms, and handyman
work. If their primary category is set to "handyman," they won't rank as well for or sell as many
of their bathroom remodels, which is the service they actually want to sell more of. The primary
category should match the service you most want to win, not just whatever feels most general.

## Q2. Have any of your clients used review-solicitation practices the new policy would now flag? What do you tell them to do instead?

Yes, he has had clients use practices he's had to pull back on. His example: asking a customer
"did you have a good experience? If so, can you leave us a good review?" His own framing was that
this is surprising to no longer be an acceptable way to ask for reviews.

**Correction confirmed by Archie, 2026-09-15.** His original example (asking only customers who
said they had a good experience) is actually a "selectively solicit positive reviews from
customers" violation, a distinct clause from on-premises pressure, verified verbatim against
support.google.com/business/answer/7400114. Reframed for the article, confirmed by Archie: ask
every customer for honest feedback, don't filter who gets asked based on how they answer a
"how was it" question first. What Google explicitly allows: "Solicit or encourage the posting of
content that does represent a genuine experience, without offering incentives to do so or
attempting to influence the rating or the contents of the review."

## Q3. Of relevance, distance, and prominence, which one do you actually see move the needle most? Real example?

Distance, in his account experience. Two real examples: his own business, Brightbox Digital, ranks
high for target keywords in the immediate area and progressively lower further out. Same pattern
observed with a Fort Wayne plumbing client, who ranks high close to his service base and lower
farther out.

**Framing confirmed by Archie, 2026-09-15.** Written as his account experience and opinion, not
asserted as a universal ranking rule, since Google doesn't publish a numeric weighting and this
reflects what he's seen in his own and one client's data, not a controlled test.

## Q4. Do you post to clients' profiles regularly? Have you seen it affect performance, or is it more about freshness?

He doesn't post a lot to client profiles regularly, and doesn't believe posting affects ranking. He
believes it's more about freshness and showing customers/prospects that the business is active.

## Q5. Any Fort Wayne-specific pattern worth calling out?

Many Fort Wayne business owners aren't deeply knowledgeable about marketing/SEO, try to learn and
set things up themselves, and miss the "ins and outs." That's the gap Brightbox's management
service fills, so owners can focus on running their business instead of learning GBP mechanics
themselves.
