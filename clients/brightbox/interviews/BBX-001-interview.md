## Questions outstanding

Short bullet answers are fine. Answer what you can, skip what you cannot substantiate.

**1. The threshold question. This is the one the whole article turns on.**
A business owner tells you they can spend $500 a month, about $16 a day, on Google Ads in Fort Wayne.
What do you actually tell them? Is that enough to work, and what does "work" mean at that number?
If the honest answer is "it depends," what does it depend on?

**2. What you do differently when the budget is genuinely small.**
Walk through what you set up differently for a $500/month account versus a $3,000/month account.
Match types, geography, campaign types, what you turn off first, what you refuse to run.

**3. A real account you can talk about.**
One anonymized situation where a small budget either worked or clearly did not. What the budget was,
what was broken, what you changed, what happened. Only include a result you can actually stand
behind. If you cannot substantiate the outcome, say so and describe the situation without the number.

**4. When you tell someone not to run Google Ads at all.**
Most competing articles never admit this exists. What makes you say "your budget is better spent
somewhere else," and where do you send them instead?

**5. Fort Wayne and Northeast Indiana specifics.**
Anything genuinely local: what clicks actually cost here for the trades you work with, competition
in specific service categories, seasonality. Only real observations. **Do not manufacture local
colour**, a generic article with "Fort Wayne" inserted is worse than one without it.

**6. The August 17 change, if you have looked.**
You said 6 of 10 accounts are limited by budget. Have you checked their Target CPA against actual
CPA yet, or opened the Bid Target Adjustment Tool? Even "I have not looked yet, here is what I plan
to do" is usable and honest.
