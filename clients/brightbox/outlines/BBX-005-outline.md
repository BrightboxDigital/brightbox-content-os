## Deliberate deviation from the NeuronWriter signal (flagged, not a separate stop)

About 10 of the 28 competitors compare **AI builders vs traditional drag-and-drop builders**.
"Traditional website builder" is therefore heavily weighted in NeuronWriter's H1 and H2 term lists.
That's a different decision: two DIY tools against each other. It isn't the decision this keyword
and this article are about. **I'm not building sections around "traditional builder."** It may come
up once in passing, where it's naturally relevant. Likely effect: a slightly lower NeuronWriter
score on heading terms, in exchange for a tighter intent match. "Best AI website builder" listicle
terms are left out for the same reason.

Two importance-10 topics are also dropped as off-intent: "difference between a website builder and
hosting", and "which AI model is best for website design". "Can AI replace developers" is folded
into the honest-tradeoff section.

## Competitor pattern (for avoidance, not copying)

Almost every result uses the same skeleton: what an AI builder is, what it does well, where it falls
short, cost, a hybrid approach, which to choose. Most are written by someone selling one side. AI
builder vendors include emergent.sh, rovela.ai, heyboss.ai, inminutes.app, playcode.io and b12.io.
Web agencies include vezert, sprintbuild, paypermonthwebsites.co.uk and neelnetworks. Brightbox
also sells one side, so the article has to be openly fair about when the AI builder is the right
call.

## Originality gate

**What will this article contribute that a reader can't get from the current top five?**

Gaps in the current results:
1. **No real test.** Only emergent.sh claims to have tested, and that was its own product. Nobody
   shows an actual AI-generated site for a real kind of local business, annotated with what it got
   right and wrong.
2. **No local context.** Nothing is written for a local service business whose leads come through
   Google Maps, the phone and a contact form, which is exactly Brightbox's audience.
3. **Cost comparisons are generic ranges.** Nobody prices a realistic three-year total for a small
   service business using the builders' current published plans against a real professional quote
   structure.
4. **Google's own guidance on AI content is barely cited.** Google's generative AI content page was
   updated 2026-10-01 and says AI-generated titles, meta descriptions and alt text must be
   fact-checked. Almost no competitor references it.
5. **Migration and lock-in are glossed over.** "Can I move later?" appears in the question set, but
   answers are vague.

**Original value elements committed to (need at least two):**
- **An annotated test build** (if Archie agrees, Interview Q4): generate a site in one AI builder for
  a sample local trade business, then screenshot and mark what's usable and what isn't. This would
  be an original screenshot set.
- **Archie's firsthand opinion and the repeated client question** (Interview Q1 and Q2).
- **A Brightbox decision framework:** a short "the AI builder is fine if... / hire someone if..."
  checklist built from Archie's answers, not invented.
- **An anonymized real situation** of a business that came to Brightbox from a builder site, only if
  Archie supplies one (Interview Q3).

The gate passes on the decision framework plus Archie's firsthand view, even if he doesn't do the
test build. Without the test build the article is less distinctive, but it doesn't fail the gate.
