### The recommended change, and why it is better rather than a compromise

**Reframe from "where budgets get wasted" to "does a small Google Ads budget actually work."**

This is not a retreat. It is a stronger article, for four reasons:

1. **Archie's data answers the exact question people are searching.** The screenshot he supplied
   shows a live campaign at **$16.50 per day** sitting in **"Limited by budget."** That is almost
   precisely the "$20 a day" question the SERP is asking, answered with a real account instead of an
   opinion.
2. **The waste material survives.** It becomes the *why*: the reason a small budget underperforms is
   that most of it is consumed by Display, Search Partners, homepage traffic and untracked
   conversions. Same content, better position in the argument.
3. **The August 17 hook fits better, not worse.** That change specifically hits campaigns marked
   "Limited by budget," which is the exact population this reframed article is about. Under the old
   frame it was one section among several. Now it is the natural climax.
4. **The competition is unusually weak.** See below.

## Proposed structure

Twelve headings. NeuronWriter's competitor set suggests no consolidation is needed.

### H1
**Does a Small Google Ads Budget Actually Work? What I See in Real Accounts**

### TL;DR
Four bullets. Must answer the threshold question directly, not tease it. Should state a real number
from Archie's audits and name the August 17 change.

### Introduction
- Hook: the $16.50 per day account sitting in "Limited by budget"
- The reader's decision: is my budget too small to bother, or am I doing it wrong?
- **Verified statistic:** Google's own documentation, quoted:
  "If your campaign's Target CPA is $10, but your recent actual CPA performance is $5, your campaign
  will deliver more closely to a $10 actual CPA starting August 17, 2026."
  Primary source, dated, quantitative, and no competitor is using it.
- Preview and why it matters now

### H2: What counts as a small Google Ads budget?
Answers importance 9. Define it honestly in daily and monthly terms. Anchor to what Archie actually
sees in Fort Wayne service accounts. Avoid a universal claim.

### H2: Is $10 or $20 a day enough?
**The single most important section.** Answers three importance-10 questions at once.
Needs Archie's direct answer plus the $16.50 per day screenshot as evidence.
Should be honest about when the answer is no.

### H2: Why small budgets underperform
Archie's audit data, stated with the denominator. Out of the last 10 accounts:
- 6 limited by budget
- 8 had Display and Search Partners enabled
- 7 sent ad traffic to the homepage
- 7 had no conversion tracking configured
- 2 had no negative keyword list

Include the misconfigured conversion tracking screenshot.

### H3 subsections under the above
Display and Search Partners on by default. Homepage instead of a landing page. No conversion
tracking. Negative keywords. Keep each tight, this is the *why*, not the whole article.

### H2: What changes on August 17, 2026
Confirmed, primary sourced, dated. Aimed squarely at "Limited by budget" campaigns.
State clearly what is uncertain: how much any individual account moves is unknown.
Mention the Bid Target Adjustment Tool, available since July 6, 2026.
**Never promise an outcome.**

### H2: How to make a small budget work
Practical, drawn from Archie's process. This is where `negative keywords`, `exact match`,
`keyword research`, `landing page` and `bid strategy` land naturally.

### H2: When I tell someone not to run Google Ads
The tradeoff section. Required by the originality gate. Most competing articles never say this,
which is exactly why it belongs.

### H2: FAQ
Five questions drawn from the SERP and topic matrix, not invented:
- Is $500 a month enough for Google Ads?
- What is the lowest budget Google Ads will accept?
- What is the difference between budget and bid?
- Why is my campaign marked "Limited by budget"?
- Do I need conversion tracking on a small budget?

**No FAQPage schema.**

## Statistics rule for this article

**Do not use** the 25-40% waste figures circulating in competing articles. No traceable methodology.
Also avoid the "75% of Google's revenue" claim that appears in a competitor, same problem.

**Use instead:** Google's own documented Target CPA example, and Archie's audit counts with the
denominator stated every time. "Out of the last 10 accounts I ran" is credible precisely because it
is small and honest.
