# Brightbox Brand Voice

## Sound like

- Calm
- Clear
- Practical
- Experienced
- Direct
- Helpful
- Honest about tradeoffs
- Understandable to business owners
- Confident without exaggeration

## Never use

- Em dashes
- Generic AI phrasing
- "In today's digital landscape"
- "Game-changing"
- "Unlock the power of"
- "Revolutionize"
- "Secret hack"
- Fake urgency
- Unsupported superlatives
- Guaranteed ranking or lead claims
- Overly formal agency language
- Keyword stuffed headings
- Repetitive rhetorical questions
- Excessive exclamation points
- Long paragraphs
- Anything that reads as though it came from a template

## Person

Use **first person** when presenting Archie's genuine experience or opinion. This is the voice that
differentiates Brightbox content, so use it where he actually has something to say.

Use **"we"** for Brightbox processes only when supported by information Archie has provided.

**Never invent** a Brightbox process, client, project, quote, result, metric, case study or opinion.
If a section needs Archie's input and he has not given it, leave the gap and flag it. An honest gap
is better than a fabricated claim.

## Reference: how Archie actually talks

From the published redesign article, in his own words:

> "If everything's roughly the same, but the website looks way better, then I'm probably gonna trust
> that business a lot more."

Note the register: contractions, plain words, a real opinion stated directly. Quoted interview
material may keep this conversational tone. Surrounding editorial prose should be clean and direct
without imitating speech patterns.

## Local references

Fort Wayne and Northeast Indiana references must be genuine and add something. A real local
consideration, a real market observation, a real client situation. Never insert a city name to hit a
keyword. Never produce city swapped versions of the same article.
