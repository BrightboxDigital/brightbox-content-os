AI Website Builder vs. Professional Website Development: What a Local Business Gets With Each

By Archie Brady, Brightbox Digital

TL;DR

  • An AI website builder is a reasonable choice if you're fairly comfortable with tech and you need a quick informational site of one to three pages.
  • Past that, the AI handles the easy part. The setup, the SEO basics, the domain and hosting decisions, and the time it takes to get everything right are still on you.
  • Google says AI-generated content, including page titles, meta descriptions and image alt text, should be fact-checked by a person before it goes live.
  • Moving off a builder later is harder than moving onto one. Wix sites can't be hosted anywhere else, and Squarespace only exports part of your content.
  • Many professional web designers use AI now too, me included. What you're paying for is the finished result, not the hours.

Every website builder now opens with the same pitch: describe your business, and the AI will build a website for you in a few minutes. For a lot of small business owners, that raises a fair question. Why pay a web designer when Wix, Squarespace or GoDaddy will build the site for you?

When someone brings up the Wix AI builder or any other AI website builder with me, my answer is always the same. You're welcome to use one. I mean that. But there's a lot more to a website that brings in calls than the part the AI does, and that's what this article is about. You'll see what today's AI website builders give you, who they're a good fit for, where they fall short for a business that depends on leads, what each path costs, and how hard it is to switch later.

What an AI website builder gives you right now

An AI website builder asks you a few questions about your business, or takes a written prompt, and generates a full site from it: pages, layout, starter copy and images. You then edit the result, either by hand or by asking the AI for changes.

The big platforms have all moved in this direction over the past year:

  • Wix launched Wix Harmony in January 2026, an AI builder built around an assistant called Aria that takes plain-language instructions and makes changes in the editor.
  • Squarespace's Blueprint AI generates a multi-page website with starter content, imagery and a visual direction from your answers to a few questions.
  • GoDaddy's Airo tools build sites from a chat, and in May 2026 GoDaddy brought Airo into the WordPress dashboard.

The first draft these tools produce is often good looking. That's the part they're best at. The question is what happens after the first draft.

When an AI website builder is the right call

I'm all for people trying it themselves. If they can make it work, more power to them. Not everyone who could start a business does, and not everyone is cut out to build their own website either. Some people are, and for them an AI website builder can be a good tool.

Here's where I think it fits:

  • You're at least somewhat tech savvy and don't mind learning as you go.
  • You need a quick informational site: one page, maybe two or three.

Once you're past a few pages, or the website needs to bring in calls and quote requests on its own, I don't think an AI website builder is the right tool for most owners. It isn't that the software can't make more pages. It's that everything around those pages becomes something you have to learn yourself.

Where AI builders fall short for a business that needs leads

The copy sounds right, but someone still has to check it

AI builders write confident, polished website copy about your business from very little information. Some of it will be generic, and some of it may simply be wrong: services you don't offer, a service area you don't cover, claims you can't back up.

Google's own guidance is direct about this. Its documentation on using generative AI content says it is "critical to manually factcheck and review all AI-generated content for accuracy and trustworthiness before publishing," and that this review applies to page titles, meta descriptions and image alt text too. The same page warns that generating many pages without adding value for users may violate Google's spam policy on scaled content abuse. An AI tool can produce the text, but checking it is still your job.

Setup is still on you

This is the part people underestimate. Talking to the AI is the easy step. After that you still need to set the site up properly, learn at least the basic SEO, and sort out your domain, hosting, email and business details. You can learn all of it, but none of it is instant.

"With the amount of time you spend getting stuff right, you might as well pay somebody."

That's the real tradeoff. An AI website builder saves you money up front and costs you time on everything the AI doesn't handle. If your time is better spent running your business, that math changes quickly.

Without code, you get the platform's layouts

I've taken over sites built on Wix, Squarespace and GoDaddy, and on all of them the limit is the same. Without writing code, you're limited to the sections and layouts the platform gives you. If you want a page to look or work a specific way that the builder doesn't offer, you either live with what's there or start adding custom code. Wix and Squarespace both allow that, but at that point you're doing web development to get a custom website anyway.

What each option costs

I'm not going to list builder prices here, because plan names and sale prices change often and the numbers would be out of date quickly. Check the builder's own pricing page before you decide. When you compare, look at the total, not just the monthly plan:

  • The plan itself. Free plans generally don't let you connect your own domain. Wix's own pricing FAQ says you need a premium plan for that.
  • Your domain after any free first year.
  • Any paid apps or add-ons for forms, booking or extra features.
  • Your time to set up, write, check and maintain the site.

On the professional side, here's something owners don't always expect. When I use an AI builder to get a project started, I don't charge a lot less than I normally would. You're still getting the same finished product: a site that's set up for search, talks about your business and does what you need it to do.

"Just because I'm able to get there in a quicker way now doesn't mean that the value of my work is lesser."

That's the honest way to think about the comparison. You aren't paying a designer for the hours of clicking. You're paying for a site that works for your business without you having to learn how to make it work.

Can you start with an AI builder and move later?

You can, but plan for it to be more work than you'd expect. Builders are easy to get onto and harder to get off of.

  • Wix says plainly that because it's a hosted platform, "you can't use another host for your Wix site." Moving means rebuilding.
  • Squarespace can export some content as a WordPress-format file, but only certain page types, one blog, and basic text and image blocks. Style settings and custom CSS don't come with it.
  • Your domain name is the part you can take with you. Wix, for example, lets you transfer a domain bought through it to another provider, with the standard 60-day lock after purchase or a contact change.

Starting on a builder isn't a mistake. Just go in knowing that if the business outgrows the site, the next step is likely a rebuild, not a quick move.

Do professional web designers use AI too?

Many do, and I'm one of them. I use AI builders to put together mock-ups for new clients, so they can see what their site could look like early on. I've also built my own AI builder with a point-and-click editor, so when I hand a site to a client who wants to make their own changes, they can do it without calling me for every edit.

So the choice isn't really AI or no AI. It's whether you want to do the work around the AI yourself, or have someone who already knows that work do it for you. The same question comes up with logos, and I covered it in whether an AI logo generator is worth using.

A quick way to decide

An AI website builder is probably fine if:

  • You're comfortable figuring out tech on your own.
  • You need one to three pages of basic information.
  • You have the time to check every line the AI writes and set up the domain, SEO basics and contact details yourself.

Hiring a professional web designer or web developer is probably the better call if:

  • Your website needs to bring in calls, quote requests or bookings.
  • You need more than a few pages, such as separate pages for each service.
  • You'd rather spend your time on the business than on learning website setup.
  • You want a design that isn't limited to the platform's built-in sections.

Frequently asked questions

Is an AI-built website good enough for SEO?

It can be a starting point, but an AI website builder doesn't know your services, your service area or what your customers search for. Google expects AI-generated titles, descriptions and content to be checked by a person, and the basics still have to be set up. If search is how you plan to get customers, it's worth reading what local SEO involves before deciding.

Can AI replace web designers?

It has replaced some of the slow parts of the job, like producing a first draft of a layout. It hasn't replaced knowing how to set a site up so it gets found and turns visitors into calls. The people who can do that themselves with an AI builder should. A lot of people can't, or don't want to spend their time learning it.

Can ChatGPT build my website?

ChatGPT can write copy and generate code, and some builders, including Squarespace, connect to it to start a site from a chat. You still need somewhere to host the site, a domain, and someone to check and set up everything it produces.

How do I know if I've outgrown my AI website builder?

The usual signs: you need more pages than your AI website builder handles well, you want a layout the builder won't let you make without code, or the site isn't bringing in the calls and form fills you need.

Is it cheaper to use an AI website builder?

Up front, almost always. Over time it depends on what your hours are worth and whether the site brings in work. The subscription is the visible cost. Setup, checking and maintenance are the hidden ones.

The bottom line

AI website builders are a good tool for the right person: someone who's comfortable with tech and needs a simple informational site of a few pages. For a business that needs its website to bring in leads, the AI only does the first part of the job. The setup, the checking, the SEO and the long-term flexibility are where a professional earns the fee, and that's true whether or not they used AI to get there.

Key takeaways

  • Use an AI builder if you're tech comfortable and need one to three informational pages.
  • Google wants AI-generated content, including titles, meta descriptions and alt text, fact-checked by a person before publishing.
  • Without code, builder sites are limited to the platform's own sections and layouts.
  • Wix sites can't move to another host, and Squarespace exports only part of a site, so plan on a rebuild if you outgrow one.
  • Compare the total cost, including your own time, not just the monthly plan.
