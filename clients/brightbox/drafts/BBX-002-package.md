## 1. Article summary

Google is retiring the standalone Local Services Ads dashboard and folding it into Google Ads as a
specialized Performance Max campaign, with rollout starting August 2026 for home and storefront
service categories (plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care,
roofing, pest control, moving). The article walks contractors through what's staying the same
(pay-per-lead billing, Search/Maps-only placement, keywordless targeting), what's actually changing
(the dashboard, manual bidding, budget structure, BBB callouts), the migration timeline, and the one
real action item: exporting historical performance reports before the migration date, since they
don't transfer automatically. It also tackles a real, currently unresolved question from public
discussion (does this compete with an existing standard Performance Max campaign for budget) and is
honest that Google hasn't confirmed the answer. Archie's own current management of two LSA accounts,
his practical pre-migration process, and his view on why more Fort Wayne service businesses should
be using LSA carry the first-person material.

## 4. Human readable preview

**Local Services Ads Are Moving Into Google Ads. Here's What Changes for Contractors.**

Google is retiring the standalone Local Services Ads dashboard, moving it into Google Ads as a
specialized Performance Max campaign built for pay per lead. Migration starts this month for home
and storefront service categories. Pay-per-lead billing, Search/Maps-only placement, and keywordless
targeting are not changing. What is changing: the dashboard itself, manual bidding, budget structure,
and Better Business Bureau callouts.

The article walks through the phased rollout (August 2026, late 2026, 2027), the single real action
item (export your historical performance reports, since they won't transfer automatically even
though lead history will), and a genuinely unresolved question from public discussion: whether this
competes with an existing standard Performance Max campaign for budget. Archie answers that as his
own professional read, labeled clearly as an opinion, not a confirmed fact.

Archie's own perspective runs through the piece: he currently manages LSA for two clients with no
migration date yet, his actual pre-change process (check Google's documentation first, then audit
his own setup), and his honest view that LSA is underused among Fort Wayne service businesses even
though it isn't common among his own current clients.

FAQ covers badge and review continuity (honestly flagged as unconfirmed by Google's migration
documentation, with the separate, confirmed fact that every LSA account is linked to a Google
Business Profile), pre-notice action items, pricing, the Performance Max interaction question again,
and post-migration performance expectations.
