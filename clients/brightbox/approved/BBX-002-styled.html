TL;DR
  • Google is retiring the standalone Local Services Ads dashboard and folding it into Google Ads as a specialized Performance Max campaign built specifically for pay per lead.
  • Migration starts August 2026 for home and storefront service categories in the US, including plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, and moving, then expands through 2027.
  • Pay-per-lead billing, Search and Maps-only placement, and keywordless targeting all stay the same. What's discontinued: manual bidding, weekly budgets, Better Business Bureau callouts, and the dashboard itself.
  • The one real action item: your historical performance reports will not transfer automatically. Export them before your migration date arrives.

01 — What's happening

A Real Change, Rolling Out This Month

Google says it plainly in its own migration documentation: Local Services Ads, LSA for short, is moving into Google Ads as "a specialized Performance Max campaign type optimized specifically for pay-per-lead goals." The rollout starts this month, for exactly the kind of businesses I work with: plumbers, HVAC companies, electricians, roofers, and the rest of the home service trades.

I currently run Local Services Ads for two clients myself, and neither has received a migration date yet. So if you're reading this because you got a notice, you're ahead of me. If you're reading it because you saw the headline and got nervous, you're in the same position I am: watching this roll out and getting ready before it lands rather than reacting after it does.

Here's what's changing, what isn't, and what to do about it now.

02 — Start with the reassurance

What's Staying the Same

Most of the core mechanics of Local Services Ads are not going anywhere.

You still only pay for a real lead. Google's documentation is direct about this: "You still only pay for valid leads (such as phone calls and messages) rather than ad clicks." That pay-per-lead model is the whole reason Local Services Ads works differently than a standard search campaign, and it carries over intact.

Your ads still show in the same two places. Google's own words: "Your ads in Performance Max campaigns with pay-per-lead goals will continue to show exclusively on Google Search and Google Maps," and "Your campaign remains keywordless." That second point matters more than it sounds. A normal Performance Max campaign reaches customers, in Google's own description, "on Search, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, Display and Discovery." This one does not. Despite carrying the Performance Max name, it is scoped to stay where Local Services Ads has always lived.

The details that make your listing work stay put. Google confirms your "targeted locations, service types, business categories, budget limits, ad schedule, and business photos will move over seamlessly without requiring your manual input." You are not rebuilding your listing from scratch.

03 — What's different

What's Changing

The standalone Local Services Ads dashboard is going away. Everything moves into the regular Google Ads interface, managed alongside any other campaigns you run there.

A few specific mechanics change with it, according to Google's documentation:

  • 01 Manual bidding is no longer supported. If you've been setting your own bids, that control goes away with the migration.
  • 02 Weekly budgets convert to daily average budgets. If you've been thinking in weekly terms, plan to think in daily ones instead.
  • 03 Better Business Bureau callouts are no longer supported. If that badge mattered to your listing, it will not carry forward in the same form.

04 — The rollout

Who Gets Migrated First, and When

Google has published a phased timeline rather than a single cutover date:

  • Aug 2026 "home and storefront service advertisers in the United States (including plumbing, HVAC, electrical, appliance repair, house cleaning, lawn care, roofing, pest control, and moving)."
  • Late 2026 "service-area businesses without physical storefronts and accounts with custom bidding or booking configurations."
  • 2027 "Non-U.S. accounts, and all remaining business categories."

Google says you'll get email notice 14 days before your actual migration date, followed by a final reminder 7 days after that. Once the migration happens, Google's guidance is to "allow up to two weeks for the migration process to fully complete and for your campaign performance to ramp back to stable levels." A short dip or fluctuation in that window would be consistent with what Google itself describes, not necessarily a sign something went wrong.

05 — Act on this now

The One Thing to Do Before Your Migration Date

This is the part worth acting on now, whether or not you've received a notice yet.

Important Google states directly that your previous campaign-level performance metrics, meaning past impressions, clicks, weekly spend, and ad-level performance reports, will not migrate to Google Ads. Your lead history is different. Google says that transfers automatically. It's the reporting history around how those leads were generated that disappears unless you save it yourself first.

My own process when a client's account structure is about to change starts in the same place every time: I check Google's own documentation first to see exactly what's happening, rather than going off a headline or a secondhand summary. Then I go back through my own account setup and process to see what needs to change on my end before the platform changes it for me. That second step is the one that catches problems, because Google's documentation tells you what's changing in general, not what matters for your specific account.

For LSA specifically, that means before your migration date arrives:

  • 01 Export or screenshot your current performance reports, at the campaign level and the ad level, going back as far as your reporting allows.
  • 02 Note your current weekly budget, since it converts to a daily average and you'll want to sanity check the conversion after migration.
  • 03 Confirm whether you were relying on manual bidding, since that control goes away.

If you manage your own Google Ads account and this is starting to feel like a lot to track on top of everything else you're running, that's exactly the kind of account change we help clients prepare for as part of ongoing account management.

06 — The open question

If You Already Run a Standard Performance Max Campaign, Does This Compete With It?

This is the question I've seen come up more than any other in the actual discussion around this change, and it's a fair one. If you're running both a standard Performance Max campaign and this new pay-per-lead version at the same time, do they compete with each other for budget?

Here's what's confirmed. This is described as a distinct, specialized campaign type, not a merger into a standard Performance Max campaign. As covered above, it keeps a separate scope entirely: Search and Maps only, versus a standard Performance Max campaign's reach across Search, YouTube, Gmail, Maps, Display, and Discovery. Structurally, that reads as two separate campaigns with two separate jobs, not one campaign absorbing the other.

Brightbox Perspective My own read, and I want to be direct that this is a read rather than a confirmed fact: because the migrated campaign is still pay per lead at its core, I don't expect it to compete with a separate standard Performance Max campaign for budget the way two similar campaigns targeting the same audience normally would. But I'll say this plainly. Google's documentation does not spell out the answer to this specific question. Until it does, or until enough advertisers running both have reported back on what they saw, treat this as an open question rather than a settled one, no matter how confidently anyone, including me, answers it.

If you're running both campaign types and want a straight answer specific to your own account rather than a general read, request a Google Ads review and we'll look at what's happening in your account instead of guessing from the outside.

07 — Where I sit

What This Looks Like From My Side of It

I'll be honest about where I sit on this. Local Services Ads aren't common among the clients I work with here in the Fort Wayne area right now. Most of the accounts I manage run standard search campaigns. But I think that's a gap, not a sign that Local Services Ads don't fit local service businesses here. The pay-per-lead model and the Google Verified Badge that comes with it are exactly the kind of trust signal that matters to someone searching for a plumber or an electrician they've never used before, and I think more local Fort Wayne service businesses should be running them, migration or not.

I don't have a real example of a client losing their reporting history because they missed an export window, and I'm not going to pretend I do. But I can tell you why it would matter if it happened. If a question ever comes up later, about what a campaign was doing three months before a slow stretch, or how performance looked before you changed something, you need something to point to. Lead history transferring doesn't help with that. The reports around it are what tell you why the leads looked the way they did, and that's exactly what Google says won't carry over automatically.

08 — Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Will my Google Verified Badge and reviews carry over?

Google's own product page for Local Services Ads highlights the Google Verified Badge as a core part of how the product builds trust with new customers. Its migration documentation, though, does not explicitly say whether that badge, or your accumulated reviews, transfer automatically as part of this specific change. It does confirm that your targeted locations, service types, business categories, budget limits, ad schedule, and business photos move over without you having to redo them. One thing worth knowing regardless of the migration: Google requires every Local Services Ads account to be linked to a public, verified Google Business Profile, and that profile is where your business details and reviews show up. If badge and review continuity matters to you, confirm it directly once your migration notice arrives rather than assuming either way.

Do I have to do anything before I've received a migration notice?

Google's documentation doesn't require any action before your notice arrives. That said, exporting your current performance reports now costs you nothing and removes the risk of missing the window later, so there's no real reason to wait for the notice to do it.

Will this cost more per lead than I'm paying now?

Google's documentation doesn't state that pricing changes as part of this migration. The pay-per-lead model itself is explicitly staying the same. Nothing in the current documentation points to a stated price increase.

Will my existing standard Performance Max campaign be affected?

Not according to what Google has confirmed so far, since the two campaign types are described as structurally distinct and scoped to different placements. Whether they interact at the budget level in practice is not something Google's documentation directly addresses. Treat it as an open question, covered in more detail above.

What happens to my leads during the migration itself?

Google's own guidance is to allow up to two weeks after migration for your campaign performance to stabilize. That's a reasonable window to expect some fluctuation, not necessarily a sign of a problem, but it's also not a guarantee that lead volume won't dip during that stretch.

Where This Leaves You

Most of what makes Local Services Ads work for a home service business isn't changing. You're still paying for real leads, not clicks. Your ads are still showing where people are searching for someone to call. The badge, the reviews, and the trust that comes with them are likely still part of the picture, even though Google's documentation stops short of confirming that explicitly.

What is changing is where you manage the account and how a few of the mechanics behind it work. The real risk isn't the migration itself. It's losing the ability to look back at how your account performed before it happened, simply because nobody exported the reports in time.
