Google Business Profile verification can be frustrating for a service business. You may not have a customer-facing storefront, permanent signs, or a front desk. Your work happens in customers' homes, at job sites, or from a vehicle.

Google's current video rules account for that. The guidance has a separate path for service-area businesses, including plumbers and locksmiths. The recording must be at least 30 seconds, and Google says its review can take up to five business days. The hard part is not making the video longer. It is showing three kinds of proof clearly in one continuous recording.

I've helped several service businesses through this process, and I've had my own verification videos fail even when I believed I showed everything. There is no checklist that guarantees approval. What you can do is make the evidence easy to follow, respond to the reason Google gives you, and ask for support if the available verification methods keep failing.

01 — The reason

Why Google Asks You to Verify

The verification process confirms that you manage or represent the business listed on Google. Once the profile is verified, you can manage its information and interact with customers from the profile.

Google chooses how you can verify your business. The options can depend on the business type, public information, region, and business hours. Different Business Profiles can get phone, email, postcard, live video, or recorded video. Some may need more than one method. If the profile only offers a recorded business video, there is no setting that lets you replace it with a postcard or text message.

That makes preparation worth doing before you start. You get one continuous take, and the proof needs to match the profile Google is reviewing.

02 — Before recording

Make the Profile and Evidence Agree

Start by reviewing the business name, address or service area, phone number, website, and primary category on the profile. Google's business guidelines say the name should match how the business is represented in the real world across signage, stationery, and other branding.

Then gather proof that uses the same business name. Depending on the business, that may include:

  • 01Business cards, invoices, permits, or a utility bill.
  • 02Branded clothing, a work vehicle, or printed marketing materials.
  • 03Tools, machines, products, or a workspace used for real jobs.
  • 04A key, locked storage area, point-of-sale system, or another business-only asset you can access.

Do not turn the video into a tour of every document you own. Pick the clearest proof and arrange it before recording. Google tells businesses not to show bank account details, tax or ID numbers, private information, or other people's faces.

03 — The checklist

The Google Business Profile Video Verification Checklist

Google's official instructions for a service-area business group the evidence into three parts. Your recording should cover all three.

Proof 01
01

Show Where the Business Operates

Record street signs, nearby landmarks, a building number, or another clear marker at the business location. This helps connect the base address to the place shown on Google Maps. Google warns against filming empty land or a place with no markers. If the business is based at your home, you may record signs and landmarks in the immediate neighborhood.

This part is about the base location, even when the address is hidden from customers. Do not start inside a garage with no outside context and assume the tools alone establish where the business is based.

Proof 02
02

Show Proof That the Business Exists

Next, show the work. Google lists professional tools, equipment, products, a workspace, business cards, and branded clothing as examples. A roofing company might show ladders, safety gear, and a branded truck. A cleaner might show labeled supplies and the equipment used on jobs. A mobile detailer might show the vehicle, tanks, hoses, and machines.

The goal is not to make the setup look large. It is to show that this is a real business equipped to perform the service named on the profile.

Proof 03
03

Show Proof That You Manage the Business

Finish with something that a customer or passerby could not normally access. Google gives examples such as performing a service, unlocking a branded van, or showing a permit, invoice, or utility bill that matches the business name.

In my own videos, I also show access to the business website's back end when it helps connect me to the business. Google does not list website administration as one of its standard examples, so I treat it as extra proof, not a substitute for the location, operating, and management evidence the instructions request.

04 — One continuous take

A Practical One-Take Recording Route

Google requires the video to be live, unique, complete, unedited, and recorded on a mobile device through the Business Profile. You cannot record it ahead of time and upload it later. Plan the route before you tap Start recording.

  • 01Begin outside with a street sign, building number, or nearby landmark.
  • 02Move to the vehicle, workspace, or storage area where the business operates.
  • 03Show tools, equipment, products, business cards, branded clothing, or signage.
  • 04Show that you control the business by unlocking the vehicle or workspace, accessing a business-only system, or displaying a matching document.
  • 05Stop after all three proof categories are clear.

Keep it short enough that the evidence is easy to follow. Short does not mean rushed. Pause long enough for the business name, street marker, and key proof to be readable. Cover what Google requests first, then show extra proof only when it makes the case clearer.

05 — Service-area businesses

Can You Verify Without Displaying Your Home Address?

Yes. Supplying an address so Google can verify the business is different from displaying that address to customers.

Google defines a service-area business as one that visits or delivers to customers but does not serve them at its own address. If that describes your business, Google's guidance says to remove the address from the public profile and list the service area instead. A plumber based at home, for example, can hide the residential address while still recording nearby signs or landmarks to verify where the business is based.

A hybrid business is different. It serves customers at its location and also visits or delivers to them. Google says a location shown as a storefront should be staffed, able to receive customers during its stated hours, and have permanent on-site signage.

Avoid the shortcutDo not use a virtual office as a shortcut. Google's guidelines say a service-area business cannot list a virtual office unless that office is staffed during business hours. Set the profile up for the business model you run, not the verification method you hope to get.

06 — After recording

What Happens After You Submit the Video?

After recording, Google lets you review the video before you upload it. Check that the location marker, business proof, and management proof can all be seen. Re-record it before uploading if one part is missing or unreadable.

Google says video review can take up to five business days. Check the profile for its verification status. A successful review produces a verification notice. If the review is not successful, Google says it will share why the video was not accepted and allow you to submit a new one.

07 — Try again with better proof

What to Do if the Video Is Rejected or Stalls

If you see Review issues, open it before recording again. Google lists three common problems: the business name was not visible, the nearby area was not shown, or proof of authorization was missing. Your next video should include everything the first video showed plus the missing evidence.

If the Get verified button comes back, Google says it could not fully verify the business. Try the method or methods shown on the profile again. If another method is available, you can use it, but Google still controls which options appear.

From my own experience, a video may be rejected repeatedly even when you believe you covered every request. I keep working through the stated issues, and if the available methods still do not work, I ask the Google Business Profile support team for help. In cases I've handled, reaching a person has often moved the process forward. That is my experience, not a promised result, and Google does not publish enough detail to say who reviews every submission.

08 — Common questions

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I Choose a Different Verification Method?

No. Google says its system automatically chooses the methods offered to your profile. If more than one option appears, you may try another available method. You cannot add an option that Google has not offered.

How Long Does Google Business Profile Verification Take?

Google says review can take up to five business days, though rare cases may take longer. For a recorded video, wait for the result and check the profile for a verification notice or Review issues message.

How Do I Know Whether My Profile Is Verified?

Google sends a confirmation email when verification succeeds. If Get verified appears again, Google says it could not fully verify the business and you should try again.

Can I Verify a Home-Based Service Business?

Yes, if the business visits or delivers to customers in person and meets Google's eligibility rules. Hide the home address from customers, set an accurate service area, and show nearby location markers plus proof that the business operates and that you manage it.

Does Google Charge for Business Profile Verification?

No. Google does not charge to verify or reinstate a Business Profile. You may choose to hire someone to help with setup or troubleshooting, but payment does not buy approval.

Is an Online-Only Business Eligible?

Not if it has no in-person contact with customers. Google's video verification guidance says an online-only business with a virtual storefront and no in-person services is not eligible for a Business Profile.

10 — The bottom line

Make the Evidence Easy to Follow

A good verification video is not the longest one. It is the one that shows the right evidence in an order a reviewer can follow: where the business operates, what it uses to do the work, and why you are authorized to manage it.

Plan the route, line up the proof, keep the recording clear, and be ready to try again if Google identifies a gap.
