# Editorial Standards

Applies to every client. Client-specific voice lives in `clients/<client>/brand-voice.md`.

## Core principles

1. Help the intended reader accomplish something meaningful.
2. Produce original value, not a rewritten summary of competitors.
3. AI assists. It does not substitute for the author's experience.
4. Accuracy beats speed.
5. Search intent beats keyword count.
6. Natural readability beats a perfect optimization score.
7. A live source is not automatically a valid source.
8. Length follows usefulness and intent, not a rigid count.
9. One excellent page beats several thin keyword variations.
10. Updating an existing page is often more valuable than creating a new one.

Do not create an article because a phrase is trending. Do not create separate articles for minor
query variations. Do not use fake local references to insert a city name. Do not produce city
swapped doorway content. Do not add unsupported AEO, GEO or AI search hacks.

## Claims that are never permitted

- E-E-A-T is a score or a single ranking factor. It is not.
- Structured data guarantees rankings or rich results. It does not.
- Indexing is guaranteed. It is not.
- Any guaranteed ranking, traffic or lead outcome.

## Required article structure

**Length target:** roughly 1,500 words. There is no mandatory count. Let intent, complexity,
competitor analysis and reader usefulness decide. Never add filler to hit a number.

### SEO metadata block

SEO title, meta description, suggested slug, primary keyword, secondary topics, search intent,
content category, suggested excerpt, author, publication date placeholder, updated date placeholder,
canonical URL placeholder.

Targets, not guarantees: SEO title around 50 to 60 characters, meta description around 140 to 155.
Never make a title awkward to hit a count. Avoid years in permanent slugs unless the year is
essential to the page's purpose.

### H1

Exactly one. Describes the article accurately and carries the primary topic naturally.

### TL;DR

Directly after the H1. Either three to five concise bullets or one short paragraph. It must answer
the central question, not tease the article.

### Introduction

A real hook, the reader's problem or decision, at least one verified statistic or quantitative fact
where defensible, a preview of what they will learn, and why it matters to this audience.
No generic scene setting.

**On the introductory statistic:** it must be relevant, accurately represented, verified, and
reasonably current. If no defensible statistic exists, say so and omit it. Never insert a weak or
misleading number to satisfy the format.

### Table of contents

Lists every H2. Must match the final structure exactly. Check this last.

### Body

Each main section answers a real reader question, explains why it matters, gives practical guidance,
includes an example where possible, and carries the author's experience where appropriate.

Use descriptive headings, short paragraphs, and lists or tables where they improve clarity.
Callouts available: Quick Tip, Important, Example, Local Consideration, Watch Out, Brightbox
Perspective. Do not overuse them. Bold conclusions and warnings, never keywords for SEO.

Avoid repetitive rhetorical questions.

### Experience and trust

Incorporate interview material: what the author commonly sees, how they evaluate the problem,
mistakes businesses make, tradeoffs, exceptions, local considerations, anonymized client examples,
screenshots, a real process or checklist.

**Never fabricate experience to make an article look more authoritative.**

### CTAs

Two to three contextual CTAs in the body, each connected to its section. Never repeat identical CTA
language. Options: request a website review, discuss an SEO strategy, get help with Google Business
Profile, request a Google Ads review, view the portfolio, contact.

### Internal links

Three to five. Validate every destination before use. Natural descriptive anchor text. Link to the
most specific relevant page. Do not link the same URL repeatedly. Do not force links. Same tab.
Also check whether existing pages should link into the new article.

### External links

Three to five authoritative links. Every one must pass claim-level validation. Place the link close
to the claim it supports. Prefer primary sources. A generic source list at the bottom does not
substitute for contextual citation. Never imply a source supports more than it does.

### Images

Two to three meaningful images. At least one should ideally be an original asset: screenshot,
diagram, checklist, workflow, annotated example, client work with permission, or an author photo.

For each: placement, purpose, concept, alt text, caption where useful, aspect ratio, filename,
source or creation method, privacy details to remove, and whether the author must supply it.

Avoid decorative stock imagery where an original instructional asset would serve the reader better.

### FAQ

Roughly four to seven, only where genuinely useful. Sources: NeuronWriter, search result questions,
Reddit, client questions, the interview. Concise but substantive answers.

**Do not create FAQPage schema.** Do not dump unused keywords into FAQs.

### Conclusion

Answers the central question, reinforces the recommendation, includes appropriate nuance, and
transitions naturally into the final CTA.

### Key takeaways

Three to five specific takeaways. Never vague. "SEO is important" is not a takeaway.

### Main CTA

Ends the article. Links to the most relevant service or contact page.

### Share buttons

Do not generate share button code inside articles. Add a publishing QA item to confirm the site
template handles it.

## HTML output rules

One H1. Logical H2 and H3 hierarchy. Semantic paragraphs, lists, tables where useful, blockquotes
where appropriate. Accessible link text.

No inline CSS. No scripts. No invented image URLs. No schema markup in the body. No unnecessary
wrapper elements. **No em dashes.**

## Structured data

Recommend or verify: Article or BlogPosting, author Person, publisher Organization, breadcrumbs where
the template supports them, image information, datePublished, and dateModified only after a
substantive update.

**No FAQPage schema.** Structured data must agree with visible page content.

## Authorship

Every article identifies the genuine author. Preferred visible author: Archie Brady.

The author area should carry his name, role, a short factual biography, a link to an author or About
page, relevant experience, the published date, and a substantive updated date where applicable.

The visible byline and the Article or BlogPosting markup must agree. Do not show a team name in one
place and a person in another unless both are genuinely credited and the distinction is explained.

## Originality review

Before approval: compare the draft against competing pages, check for suspiciously similar phrasing
or structure, confirm meaningful original value, confirm the author's experience is clearly
distinguished from sourced facts, confirm quotations are accurate, confirm no client information was
invented, confirm no competitor language was copied.

Never use an AI detector to judge quality.

- [ ] Originality gate satisfied, two or more original value elements present
- [ ] Exactly one H1

- [ ] Introduction carries a defensible statistic, or the omission is explained
- [ ] Table of contents matches final H2s
- [ ] Outline followed
- [ ] Two to three contextual CTAs
- [ ] Three to five internal links, all validated
- [ ] Three to five external links, all validated
- [ ] Claim ledger complete
- [ ] Two to three image briefs complete
- [ ] FAQs genuinely useful
- [ ] No FAQPage schema
- [ ] Conclusion present
- [ ] Three to five specific key takeaways
- [ ] Main CTA present

- [ ] No fabricated facts, clients, metrics or quotes
- [ ] No keyword stuffing
- [ ] No broken links
